RTS Beginner's Guide: How to Play Real-Time Strategy Games

By wqnlll · September 3, 2026 · 8 min read

Real-time strategy (RTS) is one of gaming's most rewarding genres, and also one of its most intimidating. There is no pause to plan your turn: the clock is always running, the enemy is always building, and a single careless minute can lose you a match. That steepness scares away many newcomers. But RTS has a hidden secret — its fundamentals are simple, learnable in an afternoon, and once you grasp a handful of habits, beating the computer becomes genuinely satisfying.

This guide is for players who have never touched the genre, or who keep losing to the AI and do not understand why. We will cover the four pillars of RTS — resources, base, units, and awareness — and end with a concrete build order you can try the moment you load a game. You can follow along in a free browser game with no download required.

Pillar one: the economy beats everything

Beginner players rush to build an army and forget to gather money. This is the most common mistake in the genre, and the easiest to fix. In almost every RTS, the economy is the entire game: every tank, every building, every upgrade is bought with the resources you harvest. A player with twice the income can out-produce and out-eat a player with a bigger army but a tiny economy.

The rule to internalize: when in doubt, gather more. Send harvesters out early, keep the collection efficient, and build enough production structures to spend what you earn. Money that sits in your bank is an army you do not have yet.

Tip: In most games, expanding your resource gathering in the first few minutes pays off more than buying a few extra starting units. Cheap harvesters produce value for the whole match; a single tank does not.

Pillar two: the base is your anchor

Your base is where your economy and production live. Lose it and you lose everything. A few habits protect it well:

  • Know your build order. Deciding your opening steps in advance — which structure, then which — keeps you from flailing. A rough plan beats improvising.
  • Do not expand blindly. Only claim a new resource spot when your current one is running low and you can defend both. Expansion without defense is just giving the enemy free resources.
  • Place defenders with intent. A couple of units covering your resource route and your production line stop cheap raids from doing real damage.

Pillar three: control your army, do not just make it

Having a big army is meaningless if you cannot aim it. The ability that separates average from strong players is unit control: selecting the right units, sending them at the right target, and not letting your whole force chase one fleeing enemy.

  • Use control groups. Bind your army (or parts of it) to a hotkey group so you can recall and re-issue orders in a second instead of dragging across the map.
  • Focus fire. Tell several units to attack the same target at once. One dead enemy at a time beats ten damaged ones — destroyed units stop shooting back.
  • Move, do not just attack. A moving army is a safe army. Use "move" orders to reposition and only give attack orders when contact matters.
  • Pull back survivors. A unit at a sliver of health is free money for the enemy. Retreat it and repair instead of sacrificing it.

Pillar four: awareness wins games

The phrase veterans use is "keep your eye on the minimap." New players fixate on the big battlefield in the center and never notice the raid sneaking toward their base on the edge. A quick glance at the map every few seconds catches approaching enemies while there is still time to react. Awareness is a skill you build with habit, not talent — and it immediately pays off.

A simple opening plan (build order) to try

Here is a forgiving, general-purpose opening you can adapt to almost any RTS, including the browser game:

  1. Gather early and hard. Immediately secure your starting resource and send out harvesters. Do not stop gathering for the whole match.
  2. Build production early. As soon as your income supports it, add a production building so you are steadily creating units rather than saving for one big purchase.
  3. Make a scouting unit. Send one cheap unit toward the enemy just to see what they are building. Knowledge of their plan lets you counter it.
  4. Advance in a wave, not a trickle. When you attack, wait until you have several units gathered, then send them together. A single wave is far more dangerous than units arriving one by one.
  5. Keep producing while you fight. The classic beginner failure: you stop making units the moment a battle starts. If you are not building, the enemy is gaining on you every second.

The bottom line

RTS feels chaotic only until you see the skeleton underneath. Gather more than you think you need, protect your base, control your army with discipline, and keep an eye on the minimap. Master those four pillars and the "impossible" AI becomes a beatable opponent. The best part?
